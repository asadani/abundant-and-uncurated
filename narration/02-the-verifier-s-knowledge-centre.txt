The Verifier’s Knowledge Centre.

Underneath the argument about regimes sits a mechanism that generalises better than either result, and it is the load-bearing wall of this essay. It also very nearly went unnoticed, for reasons worth admitting.

Continual improvement requires that data be curated in a way that injects signal exogenous to the system that produced the original data. Without such curation, performance can plateau or collapse after many iterations.

Read that carefully. It specifies exogenous signal. It does not specify human signal. And at first pass, the distinction appears to dissolve the case for the human entirely: a verifier "whether a human or a better model" is sufficient to prevent collapse. On that reading, a stronger model substitutes cleanly for a person, and the whole hand-wringing about human data is a transitional worry.

It does not substitute, and the qualification is decisive. Where a model rather than a human supplies the verification, early gains plateau and may reverse unless that verifier is perfectly reliable. More fundamentally, verifier-guided retraining ultimately drives the parameter estimate toward the verifier's own knowledge centre.

That is the whole argument in one clause. A model verifier does not add information to the system. It transfers information the verifier already holds, and then the process terminates. You can distil a stronger model into a weaker one indefinitely and never exceed the stronger one. The ceiling is not a training problem. It is an information-theoretic one.

A system that learns only from itself is not learning. It is settling.
